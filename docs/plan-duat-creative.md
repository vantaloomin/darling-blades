# Sands of the Duat - creative foundation

Companion to [plan-1.6-large-set.md](plan-1.6-large-set.md), which owns the
mechanics, the rarity skeleton, and the concretion gate. This document owns
voice, cast, names, flavor, and art direction. Where the two disagree, the
concretion draft wins on mechanics and this one wins on words and pictures.

Binding writing rules applied to every player-facing string below: no
em-dashes anywhere, no "X isn't just Y, it's Z", no rule-of-three padding, no
delve/tapestry/testament register, no title-case portentousness in prose. The
voice target is the shipped one: wry, concrete, one line, no wind-up. All
names are original; real deity names follow the Greek-set precedent
(`gk-hera`, `gk-ares`), and no real MTG or Yugioh keyword name is reused.

Working set prefix in this doc: `sd-`. Working title in player-facing copy:
**Sands of the Duat** (see Open Questions for the SET_TITLES entry).

---

## 1. Set voice

### Shop blurb (player-facing, one paragraph)

> The Duat is the road under the world, and no one walks it for nothing. The
> gods here take offerings, not prayers: lay your heart on the scale and the
> scale answers without pity and without malice. The Bastet walk the road
> anyway, armed, because they have spent lives before and know what a life
> buys. Bring gold for the river, bring iron for the gates, and know what you
> are willing to leave on the stone.

### Shop tile one-liner (the `SET_BLURBS` slot, matching the shipped format)

> The scale weighs, the river returns

Alternates if the tile reads long: "Offerings, judgment, and the road back" or
"Gold, judgment, and the long way home".

### Tagline

> **Everything comes back. Nothing comes back free.**

Alternates: "Death is a door. The door takes its price." and "She has died
before. She came back armed."

### How this set sounds different from Dark Tales and Yokai Nights

Dark Tales speaks in curses and fine print, where the danger is a promise you
did not read closely enough, and Sands of the Duat speaks in judgment, where
the danger is a truth about you that is simply read out. Yokai Nights never
stops moving, all rain and traffic and deals closing at speed, while the Duat
holds still: a hall, a beam, a feather, and a measurement that will not be
hurried or argued with. Both shipped sets keep their menace indoors and after
dark, in a manor or a club or a tower, and this one puts it in daylight hard
enough to flatten the shadows, because the worst thing here happens at noon in
front of witnesses.

Register notes for anyone writing copy in this set:

- **Judgment is played straight.** The scale, the feather, the weighing, and
  the verdict are never a joke and never winked at. Their power comes from
  being impartial: the underworld is not cruel, it is exact.
- **Money words stay when they are concrete myth.** Toll, offering, price,
  weight, debt, jar, gold. Drop anything that sounds like an office: no
  clerks, no accounts, no paperwork, no late fees, no standing orders.
- **The Bastet carry the wit, and it is dry and quiet.** They understate,
  they do not quip, and they never puncture a god's scene. Deity cards get the
  flat statement that lands harder for being flat.
- **Weight over volume.** A short declarative sentence about an inevitable
  thing beats an ornate one. No incantation register, no "delve", no chanting
  rhythm, no grandeur adjectives doing the work a fact should do.
- **Never say "soul".** The set trades in hearts, names, breath, and shadow,
  which are the concrete Egyptian units. "Soul" is the vague word that pulls
  the whole set toward generic fantasy.

## 2. The cast

Thirteen named legends form the spine: eight real-myth deity names (Greek-set
precedent; Anuket added by owner ruling 2026-08-18), four Bastet warriors
carrying original given names in the gacha register, one judge, one sun-barge
helm, and two crossover hooks into shipped sets. Every character is an adult
woman, per the global art rule.

Mechanic affinity names which mechanic the character HEADLINES, not the only
one she may carry.

### 1. Bastet, Mistress of the Ninth Return

Colors: **W/R** · Headlines: **Nine Lives** + Bastet tribal · Rarity intent: UR

The tribe's founder and its last word, who spent eight of her own lives
learning what the underworld asks of anything that walks its road. She wears a
gold ring in her ear for every life she has spent, and she does not say how
many are hidden under the hair.

Art brief: a lioness-eared warrior queen standing in the mouth of the Lion
Gate at noon, one hand still down on the flagstone from a landing, gold ear
rings catching the sun, eight scorched outlines of herself faint on the stone
behind her.

### 2. Anubis, Who Holds the Scale

Colors: **W/B** · Headlines: **Preserve** · Role: the judge / weigher ·
Rarity intent: UR

She decides nothing. She holds the beam level and lets the truth of a heart
settle where it settles, and every power in the Duat has arranged itself
around the fact that she cannot be moved. She has been offered thrones,
armies, and the sun. She holds the beam.

Art brief: jackal-eared judge in white linen and gold, both hands off the
scale, the beam dead level between a feather and a red heart, hall receding
into resin-dark colonnade behind her.

### 3. Osiris, Green After Burial

Colors: **B/G** · Headlines: **Preserve** + Nine Lives · Rarity intent: UR

The first thing that ever came back, and the reason anything else can. Her
skin took the color of the flood silt during the reassembly and she has never
bothered to change it back, since it settles arguments faster than her title
does.

Art brief: a tall green-skinned queen wrapped chest-down in bright natron
linen, standing waist-deep in a barley field growing straight out of a broken
sarcophagus lid, both arms crossed over a crook and a flail held like tools
rather than regalia.

### 4. Ammit, Under the Scale

Colors: **B/R** · Headlines: **Rite** · Rarity intent: UR

She waits below the pan for the hearts that settle heavy, and she has never
once had to move to reach one. Anubis lets the truth of a heart show itself,
and Ammit is what the truth costs.

Art brief: a composite predator woman crouched in the shadow under the scale's
pan, crocodile jaw and lion mane reading in the silhouette, only her eyes and
the wet red of her mouth catching the hall's gold light.

### 5. Neith, Who Weaves the Shroud

Colors: **U/W** · Headlines: **Preserve** + Retell · Rarity intent: SSR

She wove the first shroud, the first bowstring, and the first river channel,
and she considers all three the same craft. Everything she makes is designed
to be used twice.

Art brief: a weaver-huntress at a vertical loom strung across a reed channel,
shuttle in one hand and a hunting bow slung at her back, the half-woven linen
throwing lapis water-light back onto her face.

### 6. Ra, Helm of the Night Barge

Colors: **R/W** · Headlines: **Empower** · Role: the sun-barge figure ·
Rarity intent: UR

Twelve hours down the dark water every night, one serpent waiting in the
narrows each time, and no one anywhere who can take the oar in her place. She
has made the crossing so many times that fear has worn down into habit, and
the habit is what keeps the sun coming back.

Art brief: sun-disc-crowned helmswoman at the steering oar of a burning gilded
barge, prow-fire lighting her from below, a black serpent coil breaking the
water ahead and already being ignored.

### 7. Renenutet, Who Measures the Flood

Colors: **G** · Headlines: **Empower** ramp (`extraLandDrop`) ·
Rarity intent: SSR

She sets the height of the flood each year and tells no one what she has
decided. The whole delta reads the answer off the water line and plants what
the water will allow.

Art brief: a cobra-hooded harvest goddess standing on a flood-gauge stair with
the water at her knee, one hand flat on the stone at this year's line, green
delta and stacked granaries behind her under a hard blue sky.

### 8. Nebet, Nine Times Risen

Colors: **R** · Headlines: **Nine Lives** · Bastet warrior ·
Rarity intent: SSR

The tribe's front line, and the reason nothing in the lower halls will hold a
gate against the Bastet twice. Killing Nebet is the easy part, and everything
that has tried knows what comes after.

Art brief: a scarred Bastet spearwoman getting up off one knee mid-battle,
dust still falling around her, a thin gold seam glowing across her ribs where
the last one landed.

### 9. Kesi of the Paired Knives

Colors: **R/W** · Headlines: **twinBlades** + Nine Lives · Bastet warrior ·
**Crossover hook** · Rarity intent: SSR

Two knives, one motion, and a family story that runs east: her grandmother
crossed out of the Duat and never came back, and a whole beastkin forest still
tells the tale as a scout who wandered off after mushrooms. Kesi has heard the
Beastkin version and finds it insulting in a way she enjoys.

Crossover target: `bk-nekomata-scout` ("Nine lives, one job: find the good
mushrooms."). RULED 2026-08-18: the one-lineage claim is a RUMOR, not canon.
It lives only in the competing stories the two peoples tell about each other,

Art brief: a lean Bastet duelist mid-turn with both knives already through the

### 10. Bakhet, Gate-Warden of the Lower City

Colors: **W** · Headlines: Bastet tribal (anthem) · Bastet warrior ·
Rarity intent: SR

She holds the last gate before the halls of judgment, and nothing has come
through it unweighed in two hundred years. Gods have asked her to step aside.
She has told them where the road is.

Art brief: a broad-shouldered Bastet gate-warden planted in a sandstone
gateway with a five-row lapis collar and a grounded shield, the dead reading
as soft pale shapes stretching away behind her.

### 11. Khenut, Who Pays Before the Asking

Colors: **B** · Headlines: **Rite** · Bastet warrior · Rarity intent: SR

She gives up what she owes before anything has come to take it, which leaves
nothing in the dark with a claim on her. Every offering she lays down is
early, and every one is worth more than it had to be.

Art brief: a Bastet ritualist setting a sealed jar down on a black offering
table with the payoff still off-frame, her free hand already open and empty,
lamp-gold key light from a low brazier.

### 12. Sitra, Ferrywoman of Two Rivers

Colors: **U/B** · Headlines: **Retell** · **Crossover hook** ·
Rarity intent: SR

She works the crossing where the Duat's river runs against a colder one, and
she is the only ferrywoman the dead of both lands will follow into a boat. The
far side still owes her for a passage it asked for and has never named.

Crossover target: the Greek underworld pair `gk-hades` / `gk-persephone` and
the shipped land `ld-asphodel-meadow`. Keep it to a border and an unpaid
passage; this is a hook, not a shared storyline.

Art brief: a ferrywoman poling a reed skiff along a seam where warm gold water
meets cold grey water, gold-lit on one cheek and grey-lit on the other, pale
asphodel visible on the far bank.

### 13. Anuket, Who Runs the Cataracts

Colors: **U** · Headlines: **Retell** + the Book of the Two Ways ·
Rarity intent: UR (the mono-U face; added by owner ruling 2026-08-18)

She is the fast water between the gates: every channel the river has ever
cut is a route she still remembers, and she runs them all at speed. The
Book of the Two Ways records one river. She has counted more.

Art brief: a dark-skinned river goddess running the white water of a
cataract as if it were a stair, reed-feather crown swept back, a fanned
chart case at her hip, spray thrown gold by barge light on one side and
lapis by deep water on the other.

## 3. Naming conventions

### 3a. Per-color naming grammar

Each color owns a vocabulary and a shape. Names stay short enough to read on a
hand card and concrete enough to survive the thumbnail.

| Color | The place it names | Word bank | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **W** | the ordered necropolis: halls, gates, processions, rites | alabaster, linen, natron, censer, usher, warden, almoner, lintel, procession, seal, white crown | `[Rite office] of the [Hall]` or `[Material] [Office]`. White names a SACRED DUTY and the place it is kept. |
| **U** | the river and the Book of the Two Ways: routes, charts, memory | reed, current, channel, chart, waterclock, silt, crossing, the two ways, pilot, ferry | `[Route noun] [Navigator]` or an instruction: `Read the Two Ways`. Blue names a ROUTE or a READING. |
| **B** | the price and the wrappings: debts, resin, jars, verdicts | resin, wrappings, jar, heart, debt, toll, price, taker, beetle, verdict, silence | `[Cost noun] [Role]` or the price stated flatly: `The Debt Is Called`. Black names WHAT IS OWED. |
| **R** | the barge and the lion: fire, sun, spear, sandstorm, the serpent | prow, sun-rope, ember, mane, spear, coil, noon, wake, signal, lioness | `[Fire noun]-[Weapon or beast]` or a command: `Light the Prow`. Red names an ACTION AT SPEED. |
| **G** | the flood and the reed field: silt, granary, cobra, palm | silt, flood, levee, granary, furrow, reed, scarab, palm, harvest, measure | `[Flood noun] [Growth role]` or a fact about the water: `The Flood Arrives Early`. Green names WHAT THE WATER DID. |

Two hard rules across all five: no name uses a hieroglyph transliteration a
player cannot pronounce, and no common carries a deity name (deity names are
reserved for legends, so the collection filter stays meaningful).

### 3b. Class signals

- **Bastet cards** carry exactly one feline word in the first two words of the
  name (Whisker, Sand-Pawed, Lion-Gate, Pride, Prowler, Litter, Claw, Collar)
  or, from uncommon upward, an original given name plus a title. A Bastet
  common never uses a deity name and never uses "cat" as the bare word.
- **Rite cards** name WHAT IS GIVEN or WHO TAKES IT, never the reward. The
  body on the card is the reward, so the name stays on the cost side: `Feed
  the Devourer`, `Two for the Ferrywoman`, `Give It the Better One`. Verbs are
  plain and physical (feed, give, bring, lay down, hollow), never sacrificial
  pageantry and never commercial.
- **Preserve cards** name a THING KEPT, with "second", "again", "twice",
  "kept", "wrapped", "salted", or "linen" carrying the rebirth: `Second
  Wrapping`, `The Copy Kept in Linen`, `Twice-Wrapped Champion`. Preserve owns
  the wrapping vocabulary exclusively.
- **Nine Lives cards** name a COUNT or a PERSISTENCE, never a resurrection:
  `Ninth-Life Duelist`, `Still Standing at Dusk`, `Count Again`, `She Gets
  Up`. Nine Lives never touches the wrapping words, so the two return
  mechanics stay separable at a glance.
- **Empower cards** name SCALE: deeper, higher, fuller, more, this year's
  line. Green Empower talks about the flood; red Empower talks about the sun.
- **Duals** are two-or-three-word place names in the shipped register
  (`Moonlit Marsh`, `Beacon Ridge`, `Asphodel Meadow`): terrain first, no
  adjective stacking, no character reference.

## 4. Flavor samples

Fifteen lines in the shipped voice, tuned to mythic weight per the owner's
2026-08-17 ruling. Six are attached to named cast members. Every line is one
sentence or two short ones, concrete, and lands on a fact rather than a
flourish. The Bastet lines keep a dry, understated wit; the deity and judgment
lines are played straight.

| Card | Archetype | Flavor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bastet, Mistress of the Ninth Return | bastet-tribal | She has died eight times. She remembers what each one cost. |
| Anubis, Who Holds the Scale | preserve-value | The scale is honest. Nothing else in the hall is. |
| Ammit, Under the Scale | rite-engine | She is not the punishment. She is what is left after it. |
| Ra, Helm of the Night Barge | empower-ramp | Twelve hours down the dark water. One serpent. Every night. |
| Renenutet, Who Measures the Flood | empower-ramp | The flood is generous. It has never been generous to anyone in particular. |
| Nebet, Nine Times Risen | nine-lives-aggro | Killing her is the easy part. |
| Sitra, Ferrywoman of Two Rivers | utility | Two rivers, one crossing, and a passage the far bank has never paid for. |
| The Offering Table | rite-engine | It asks once. What comes after is not asking. |
| Feed the Devourer | rite-engine | Bring something you will miss. She can tell. |
| Second Wrapping | preserve-value | The linen remembers the shape. The shape remembers the war. |
| Back on Her Feet | nine-lives-aggro | The notch in her ear says this was the last one. She has heard that before. |
| Lion-Gate Standard | bastet-tribal | Every cat in the necropolis knows that banner. So do the jackals. |
| The Weighing Hall | dual-cycle | Everything that walks in is weighed. Not everything walks out. |
| Empty Every Jar | utility | Nothing here was lost. Nothing here is where it was left. |
| Whisker-Count Scout | bastet-tribal | She counts the guards, then counts the ways past them. |

Voice notes for whoever writes the remaining flavor:

- Length ceiling is roughly 14 words. Anything longer stops reading at card
  scale and starts sounding like narration.
- Set a plain fact, then let the second clause reveal what the fact means. The
  turn is where the weight lands.
- Deity and judgment lines state something inevitable, flatly. Bastet lines
  state something practical, dryly. Neither one quips.
- Nothing in this set is described as a job, a schedule, a fee, or an
  arrangement. The underworld is a place with laws, not an institution with
  procedures.
- Never end on an ellipsis and never end on a question.

## 5. Art direction

Everything here sits underneath [art-bible/index.md](art-bible/index.md),
which stays binding: crisp cel-shaded gacha anime splash art, house anime
face, adult women only, one key plus one rim, fully rendered scenic
background, generous headroom, species tells stated explicitly, and the
NO-TEXT hard rule. This section adds only what is specific to the Duat.

### 5a. Palette

Base palettes stay keyed to color identity from `PALETTES` in
`src/art/PlaceholderArtGenerator.ts`. The set accents layer on top of them,
the way Wei's lapis-and-bronze or Greek's marble-and-gilt do.

| Accent | Hex | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tomb gold | `#e8c95a` / `#c9a84c` | collars, leaf, barge fittings, scale beam |
| Lapis | `#1f3f7a` / `#2f5fae` | inlay, water, sky, the cool rim |
| Sandstone | `#d8b98a` / `#a8834f` | architecture, the ground, dust |
| Natron white | `#f4efe2` | linen, wrappings, the Preserve copy |
| Resin brown | `#4a2f16` | shadow, embalming vessels, tomb interiors |
| Barge fire | `#ff8a3d` / `#ffd07a` | the sun disc, prow fire, brazier practicals |
| Malachite | `#2f8f6a` | jewelry inlay only, and Osiris's skin |
| **Heart-red** | `#c8253a` | **reserved** |

**The reserved-hue rule.** Heart-red appears only on the weighed heart, on
blood, and on Ammit's mouth. It appears nowhere else in the set: not on cloth,
not on flowers, not on sunsets, not as a generic red accent. It is the one
saturated cool-red in a palette otherwise built from warm golds and cool
blues, so wherever it lands the eye goes immediately, and the set gets a
free visual grammar for "this is the thing being judged" at thumbnail scale.
Red-color-identity cards use barge fire, not heart-red.

**The value floor.** Yokai Nights, Dark Tales, and Nocturne Manor all pushed
their darkest step to near-black, and three consecutive night sets have
trained the catalog toward holes on a dark UI. In this set the darkest value
in the visible band is resin brown `#3a2a1c` and never true black. If a scene
needs depth, it gets it from lapis shadow, not from black. This is the single
biggest lever separating Duat thumbnails from the three sets before it.

### 5b. Lighting: how the underworld stays readable on a dark UI

- **Key light is always a practical inside the frame**: a brazier, an oil
  lamp, the sun disc, the barge fire, a shaft through a roof slot. Never an
  unmotivated ambient. Naming the source in the entry's Lighting field is
  mandatory, and the holo shaders depend on it.
- **Rim light is lapis-cool** off the river, the night sky, or an inlaid
  surface. Warm key plus cool rim is the set's default pair, inverted from
  Yokai Nights's cool key plus hot rim, which keeps the two sets apart at a
  glance.
- **Every illustration carries a warm value above roughly 70 percent
  luminance somewhere inside the central band** (y 167 to 633 on the 640x800
  deliverable). Usually that is gold on a collar, the lit side of a face, or
  linen. Cards that fail this read as dark rectangles on the battlefield.
- **Daylight cards are allowed and encouraged.** Roughly a third of the set
  should be hard noon exteriors: white stone, short shadows, heat shimmer.
  The contrast between hard noon and lamp-lit tomb interior is the set's
  visual rhythm.
- **The NO-TEXT rule bites hardest here.** Egyptian visual language is made of
  glyphs, and generation backends will stamp hieroglyph walls, cartouches,
  and papyrus columns onto every prompt unless blocked. Binding rule for this
  set: **every wall register, cartouche, stela, papyrus, and seal renders as a
  blank gold or lapis panel with geometric banding, never glyphs.** Carry it
  on every prompt as both a positive cue ("blank gold cartouche panels,
  geometric banded registers") and a negative block ("no hieroglyphs, no
  glyphs, no writing, no inscriptions"). QA zooms every wall panel.

### 5c. Composition by mechanic family

**A Rite scene shows the payment, never the payoff and never the killing.**
Two figures minimum: one giving, one taking. The given thing is a specific
identifiable object already out of the giver's reach (a sealed jar, a heart in
a dish, a soldier's shield laid down, a spear surrendered). The taker's hands
are the focal point. The card's own body IS the reward, so the reward never
appears in the art. No gore, no on-camera sacrifice, no altar blood: the dread
in this set comes from a price being paid without protest, and the moment the
offering leaves the hand is heavier than the knife would be.

**A Nine Lives scene shows the second landing, not the death.** She is coming
up: off one knee, off the flagstones, out of a crouch, weight already
transferring forward. Dust or grit still falling sells the beat. Two things
must be in frame: the **life mark**, already visible, and a **trace of where
she went down** (a scorched silhouette on the stone, her own dropped weapon, a
cracked flagstone, a wet outline). Without the trace it is just a character
standing up.

The life mark is canon and must be stated per entry as exactly one of:

1. a **thin gold seam** across skin or armor where the wound was, lit like
   kintsugi, or
2. a **notch in one ear** with a plain gold ring through it.

Legends may carry multiple ear rings, one per life spent. Nothing else in the
set uses that ring shape, so the mark stays unambiguous.

**A Preserve scene shows the copy, not the corpse.** Two identical silhouettes
in different light: the original low and in resin-brown shadow, the token copy
standing in natron white and gold, occupying the space the original knelt in.
The copy's linen is clean and bright; that brightness is the mechanic. The
underworld's dead are polished, not decayed, so there is no rot, no
exposed bone, no flies, no green skin (malachite green is Osiris only, and on
her it is an honor). If a Preserve card shows only one figure, the second
must be implied by a shadow, a reflection, or an empty wrapping still holding
its shape.

### 5d. Bastet costume and silhouette language

Species tells, stated explicitly on every prompt because tails are the known
trap:

- **Ears**: two upright cat ears, tufted, set high on the skull. Matches the
  Nekomata row of the Beastkin species sheet, which is what makes the Kesi
  crossover legible.
- **Tail**: exactly ONE unforked tail with exactly ONE tip, emerging from the
  base of the spine at the tailbone, centered on the lower back, never from
  the hip, side, or waist. QA zooms base and tip on every Bastet card.
- **Eyes**: slit vertical pupils, amber or lapis. Short fangs visible on open
  expressions.
- **Feet**: wrapped ankles, bare soles. Nothing in the tribe wears boots. This
  is a fast silhouette read at battlefield scale and it separates Bastet from
  every human soldier in the set.

Costume:

- **Silhouette shape**: a hard triangle, wide at the shoulder and collar,
  narrow at the waist, with the tail providing the diagonal that breaks it.
  Bastet read as top-heavy; the necropolis officials read as columnar; the
  gods read as symmetrical. Three shapes, three factions, no overlap.
- **The collar is the tribe's signature.** A broad usekh collar in
  alternating gold and lapis rows, sized to read at the 119x86 battlefield
  crop. The row count is the artist's choice per composition (owner ruling
  2026-08-18: the earlier lapis-row rarity badge is vetoed; no art-side
  rarity binding).
- **Body**: pleated linen shendyt or wrap-kilt over a bound chest wrap, bare
  midriff and forearms, copper or bronze bracers, leather harness for blades.
  Fanservice-forward but heroic per the global rule: the governing read is
  battle-readiness.
- **Hair binds LOW.** No towering headdresses on Bastet cards. Cat ears
  already spend the vertical silhouette budget, and the headroom rule is
  sacred, so the tribe wears a single weighted braid, a beaded net that hugs
  the skull, or a short crop. Deities may have tall crowns; the tribe may not.
- **Bodies are designed per character**, not defaulted: build, height
  impression, and skin tone stated explicitly in every entry, with the tribe
  spanning the full range of warm browns.

### 5e. Dual-cycle landscape briefs

Landscape rules from [land-art.md](land-art.md) apply: no characters, no
faces, terrain in the central band, painted anime environment idiom, coherent
bleed above and below.

1. **The Weighing Hall (W/B)**: an alabaster judgment hall with a pale
   unbroken line of the dead running out the door, one empty gold scale
   glowing at the far end, resin dark colonnade closing in from both sides.
2. **Emberwake Channel (U/R)**: a narrow black-water channel still carrying
   the sun barge's burning wake, lapis water throwing orange back onto
   sandstone canyon walls.
3. **Silt-Tomb Terrace (B/G)**: stepped tomb terraces half swallowed by green
   flood silt, palm shoots pushing up through cracked door seals.
4. **Noon Barge Landing (R/W)**: a white stone quay under vertical noon light
   with almost no shadow, the barge's gilded prow just touching the stone,
   heat shimmer rising off it.
5. **Reedway Delta (G/U)**: a braided delta of reed channels seen from the low
   bank, malachite reeds against a flat lapis sky, one distant empty skiff.

### 5f. Booster pack art concept

Following the shipped set-scoped booster pattern: one pack face, one set
symbol, no lettering anywhere.

**Concept: the sealed door.** The pack face is a vertical lapis panel framed
by a gold cartouche border that is deliberately **blank**, with Bastet's
profile rendered in flat gold leaf as a silhouette, backed by a rising sun
disc in barge-fire orange. Sandstone texture shows through at the panel edges.
The pack's tear line runs across the middle of the seal as a hairline crack,
so the seal reads as intact until it is not. Opening splits the seal, and the
open animation throws natron dust and gold motes through the barge-fire glow.

Notes for the pack artist: keep the composition symmetrical (the tribe reads
top-heavy, the gods read symmetrical, and a pack is a god-tier object); keep
the palette to exactly three values of lapis, two of gold, one of fire; and
put no heart-red on the pack at all, so the reserved hue stays reserved for
the cards.

## 6. Owner rulings and remaining questions

### Resolved 2026-08-17

1. **Tone: mythic weight, institutional comedy is out.** RULED. Judgment and
   the scale are played straight; the Bastet keep a dry, understated wit in
   the shipped voice. Office vocabulary (clerks, accounts, late fees, standing
   orders, queues, receipts, paperwork) is banned from every player-facing
   string. Concrete myth money words survive: toll, offering, price, weight,
   debt. Applied throughout section 1 (blurb, tile line, register notes, the
   comparison paragraph), the cast identity lines, six card names in sections
   3c and 3d, all fifteen flavor lines, and two art briefs. The tagline
   "Everything comes back. Nothing comes back free." survives the shift
   unchanged.
2. **Ammit as a predator woman.** RULED: confirmed. She stays an adult woman
   with crocodile and lion features carried in the silhouette, so the global
   "every character is an adult woman" rule holds with no catalog exception.
3. **Bastet naming.** RULED: confirmed. The card is "Bastet, Mistress of the
   Ninth Return" in full title form, and the tribal Axis stays "Bastet".
4. **Dual-cycle pairs.** RULED: confirmed as the five enemy pairs (W/B, U/R,
   B/G, R/W, G/U). The five landscape briefs in 5e stand as written.
5. **"Nine Lives" as a printed MTG card name.** RULED: acknowledged, mechanic
   name stands. Consequence held: no CARD in this set is named "Nine Lives".
6. **Set title and prefix.** RULED: "Sands of the Duat" ships as-is with
   prefix `sd-`. This is now a build-time check, not a taste question: verify
   the shop tile does not physically clip the title at the shipped tile width,
   and only revisit if it does.
