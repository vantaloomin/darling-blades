# Patch 1.8: Drowned Deep

> *"The harbour floor is not the bottom. It is her brow."*
> Dagon, the Deep Itself

252 new cards, a tenth world, two new bosses at the top of the tower, and a
new kind of card that does something every single turn. Also: 27 lands that
nobody could play have been hauled out of the binder and given jobs.

## In this patch

- **New set:** Drowned Deep, 252 cards, with **Whispers** and **Tithe**
- **New across the whole game:** **Duty**, the tap ability
- **27 unplayable lands** are now playable artifacts
- **The tower climbs to 26**, and six older bosses were rebuilt
- **Opponents got a lot smarter**
- **Anonymous play stats**, with the off switch shown to you first
- **The game opens in about a second** instead of after every card has loaded
- Art, fixes, and the usual sweeping up

## New set: Drowned Deep

Dunmarrow is a fishing town on a cold coast, built on the spires of something
older. The lighthouse has not gone dark in three hundred years. The tide comes
up one step a generation. Below the harbour floor the old architecture keeps
going down, further than any diver has come back from, and from down there
comes the Deep.

The Deep is not a monster. It is a chorus. It speaks in the voices of the
drowned, and the awkward part is that what it offers is real.

All 252 cards carry finished art. The set has its own boosters, its own shop
deck, and four new tokens.

### Whispers

> When a card goes to your graveyard from your hand or your deck, you may cast
> it from there for its Whispers cost, until your opponent's next Dawn.

Milled it? Discarded it? The Deep hands it back, cheaper and stranger.

| Card | You pay | Whispered | It does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Drawn Under** | {2}{U}{U} | {1}{U} | Return target creature to its owner's hand, then draw 2 cards |
| **Storm Surge** | {1}{R}{R} | {R} | 4 damage to a creature, then 2 to your opponent |
| **The Tide That Remembers** | {3}{U}{U} | {2}{U}{U} | Draw 4, then mill 3, which is a polite way of saying "set up three more Whispers" |

*"The surge takes the wharf, the boats, and the argument about whose fault it
was."*

### Tithe

> You may sacrifice any number of creatures you control as you cast this. It
> costs one less for every two points of their combined Defense. Coloured
> mana is still paid.

Horrors are expensive. Your own creatures are, regrettably, a currency.

- **Mother Hydra, Queen Beneath Dunmarrow**, 6/7 Dreaded. *"She has been the
  town's landlord for three hundred years and has never once raised the
  rent."*
- **Tidepool Colossus**, 6/7 Overrun. *"It fits in a tidepool the way a
  cathedral fits in a town: badly, and it does not care."*
- **Dagon, the Deep Itself**, 8/8 Dreaded and Overrun, for the player who
  looked at a board full of tokens and saw a payment plan.

## New mechanic: Duty

A Duty is an ability you use by **tapping the card**, during your Morning or
Afternoon. Some cost mana too, and the cost reads the way you expect:

> `{2}, {T}: Foresee 3.`

One use a turn, every turn, for as long as the card survives. **82 cards**
carry a Duty, in Drowned Deep and in older sets alike, on creatures, artifacts
and enchantments. A card cannot use its Duty the turn it arrives, unless it has
Warcry.

A few of the people you will be meeting:

- **Agathe Vane, the Salt Widow**. `{T}: Your opponent loses 1 life, then you
  gain 1 life.` *"Four husbands, one wedding ring, and the sea owes her for
  all of them."*
- **Isolde Marrow, Drowned Cartographer**. `{1}, {T}: Foresee 2, then draw a
  card.` *"Her charts are accurate to the inch, for a coast that no longer
  exists."*
- **Constance Reyne, Harbourmaster**. `{2}, {T}: Create a 1/1 Lantern Wisp
  with Skyborne.` *"She logs every boat that leaves and every boat that
  returns. The ledgers do not match."*

## The Great Land Reclamation

Ever since the Warchest arrived, a land that was neither a basic nor a dual
could not go in any deck. Twenty-seven commons across five sets have been
sitting in your binder ever since, looking scenic and doing nothing.

They have been given jobs. Each is now an **artifact with a Duty**, in the
colour it used to tap for, with the same art.

| It was | It is now | Its Duty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mist Road | **Mist-Road Waymark** | `{T}: Foresee 1` |
| Palace Steps | **Glass Slipper** | `{1}, {T}: You gain 2 life` |
| Red-Roof Village | **Festival Rocket** | `{2}, {T}: Deal 2 damage to target creature` |
| Moor Path | **Moorlight Lantern** | `{3}, {T}: Your opponent loses 2 life, then you gain 2 life` |
| Riverbend Trail | **Riverbend Waterwheel** | `{3}, {T}: Return target creature card from your graveyard to your hand` |
| Interstellar Crossing | **Crossing Beacon** | `{3}, {T}: Draw a card` |
| Court of Whispers | **Listeners' Curtain** | `{T}: Put the top card of your deck into your graveyard` |

The Festival Rocket keeps its old flavor text, which turns out to have been
foreshadowing: *"The roofs are red from paint, weather, and one regrettable
festival."* Court of Whispers was renamed because Whispers is a mechanic now,
and it does not have it.

**Every copy you own converted by itself.** Nothing was taken from your
collection. They open from packs again, turn up in drafts, and can be crafted.

> **Heads up, completionists.** Those 27 cards count toward their sets again,
> so four set-completion goals got longer: **Silver Veil** 81 to 84, **Grail
> Oath** 78 to 83, **Nocturne Manor** 78 to 83, **Dark Tales** 172 to 180. If
> you had one of those sets finished, you are a few commons short of it now.
> They are commons, and they are in the packs. We are sorry, and also a
> little bit not.

## Boss tuning

Here is the uncomfortable thing we learned this patch. When the opponents got
smarter (next section), several bosses got *worse*. They had been winning
partly because the decks facing them kept making mistakes, and once the
mistakes stopped, the holes in their lists showed. So we went and found the
holes.

Win rates are each boss, on Hard, against the five starter decks over 1,000
measured games.

| Rung | Boss | Before | After | What was actually wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | **Brunhild** | 70% | 76% | Her Valkyries had no burn to clear the way |
| 19 | **Queen of the Lanterned Roof** | 59% | 72% | She had nothing to play before turn three |
| 20 | **Kitsune Neon Tyrant** | 82% | 88% | A conversion error had quietly deleted her four best cards |
| 22 | **Bastet** | 62% | 74% | Opponents learned to block her Twin Blades properly |
| 23 | **Chrome Broodmother** | 60% | 72% | She could not block a single flier. Crimson Muster has twelve |
| 25 | **The Drowned Deacon** | 36% | 66% | Shipped rough, tuned up before release |

> **Developer's note.** Every one of these was a measured change. A candidate
> list had to win on the five starter decks *and* hold up against fourteen
> decks before we kept it. Twice this patch a change looked like a five point
> gain on the small test and turned out to be nothing on the big one, and we
> threw it away both times. One boss, the Marsh-Mother, got the same
> treatment and came out needing no change at all.

Every boss from rung 14 up now clears her floor with a full margin. Eight
floors were raised, and the two new bosses took their first.

## Opponents play the whole card now

We audited everything the AI does and rebuilt how it reads the game. Forty
written-down behaviours are now tested, and all forty pass.

**Things the AI used to do, and no longer does:**

- Choose many of its spells by price tag. Nine different board wipes were
  being cast on schedule, including into the AI's own winning board.
- Leave 41 Charms in hand for entire games, because it had no idea what they
  were for.
- Block a 2/1 with Twin Blades using a 2/2, and watch the 2/2 die without
  landing a hit.
- Fail to pass. Hard could not decline to act, even when acting was worse.
- Sacrifice only creatures that were already damaged when paying a Tithe.
- Ignore the moment a Hauntlink could be moved to safety.
- Draft without reading mechanics at all.

**What it does now:** reads what a spell does before casting it, knows when
passing is right, fights correctly around Twin Blades, Sentinel and First
Blade, sacrifices sensibly, moves a link to the creature that wants it, and
holds mana for a Duty worth waiting for. Draft opponents read every mechanic
on a card when they pick.

Every difficulty still plays through the same hidden-information view you do.
None of them can see your hand. They just stopped wasting theirs.

## Anonymous play stats, off in one tap

This one we will say plainly, with no jokes.

Starting with 1.8 the game can send short anonymous summaries of how it is
played: which formats and colours get played, how long duels run, which cards
see play. Every number is rounded into a broad range before it leaves your
device. There is no name, no account and no identifier, so nobody, including
us, can tell which summaries came from you.

- **You are told first.** The first time you reach the main menu, the game
  tells you this, with the switch right there, before anything is sent.
- **What is sent** lists every single field, in the game.
- **Off means off.** When the switch is off, the game makes no requests to the
  stats service at all.
- The privacy policy is linked from the same panel.

**How we read those numbers:** as a rough guide, never as proof. The stats
service is public and can be spammed, and ad blockers drop some share of web
reports and none on desktop, so the two are not comparable. Card changes are
still decided the way they always have been, by thousands of measured matches
between the game's own opponents.

## The menu comes first now

Until this patch the game loaded every card illustration it owns, all 1,537
of them, before it would show you the main menu. On a fast machine that was
sixteen seconds of "Unsheathing Blades"; over a slow connection it could be a
minute and a half. The menu itself draws no cards at all.

Now the menu opens in about a second, and the cards load behind it, the ones
you are about to need first: the tutorial's, then the starter decks', then
your own. A screen that needs cards you do not have yet shows the same
loading line until they arrive, which for a duel or a pack is a fraction of a
second. The first time you open the full collection in a session it may still
ask you to wait for the rest.

## Art

**Spell art shows her face.** Art for spells, artifacts and enchantments was
being cropped around the middle of the picture, which on 26 cards cut the face
clean out of the card window. The crop now finds the subject first.
Twenty-nine cards were reframed from their original illustrations and four
were repainted.

## Bug fixes

- Fixed an issue where a chain of triggers that fed itself could lock a duel
  forever. It now stops quietly at a sensible depth and lets you get on with
  your evening.
- Fixed an issue where opening an older save in a newer version could reset a
  few one-time settings, such as the Darlings tutorial flag and a deck's
  chosen card back. Saves now keep them.
- Fixed an issue where one card could not carry both Whispers and Tithe.
  Cinderjaw, the Fire That Swims, now has both, and is delighted.
- Fixed an issue where the desktop app logged two errors every time it sized
  its own window. Nothing was visibly broken. It was just complaining.
- A paid Duty now prints its mana before the tap symbol, `{2}, {T}:`, the way
  every card player reads it.
