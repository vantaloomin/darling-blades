Drowned Deep is here: 252 new cards, a tenth world, and cards that do
something every turn.

**A new expansion from under the harbour.** Dunmarrow is a fishing town built
on the spires of something older, and the tide comes up one step a
generation. The Deep is not a monster. It is a chorus, it speaks in the voices
of the drowned, and what it offers is real. Two mechanics carry it.
**Whispers**: when a card goes to your graveyard from your hand or your deck,
you may cast it from there for its Whispers cost, until your opponent's next
Dawn. **Tithe**: sacrifice your own creatures as you cast a Horror, and it
costs one less for every two points of their Defense. All 252 cards carry
finished art, and the set has its own boosters and four new tokens.

**Cards with a Duty.** A Duty is an ability you use by tapping the card,
during your Morning or Afternoon, once each turn. Some cost mana as well, and
the cost reads the way you expect: `{2}, {T}: Foresee 3.` Eighty-two cards
have one, in Drowned Deep and in older sets alike, on creatures, artifacts
and enchantments.

**Twenty-seven lands nobody could play are now cards you can.** Since the
Warchest arrived, a land that was neither a basic nor a dual could not go in
any deck. Those 27 commons, from Silver Veil, Grail Oath, Nocturne Manor, Dark
Tales and Starborne, are now artifacts with a Duty. Mist Road is the
Mist-Road Waymark, Red-Roof Village is the Festival Rocket, Interstellar
Crossing is the Crossing Beacon. Every copy you own converted by itself, with
the same art, and nothing was taken from your collection. They open from
packs again, turn up in drafts, and can be crafted.

**Four set-completion goals got longer, and nothing was lost.** Because those
27 cards count toward their sets again, the completion targets rose: Silver
Veil from 81 to 84, Grail Oath from 78 to 83, Nocturne Manor from 78 to 83,
and Dark Tales from 172 to 180. If you had one of those sets finished, you
are a few commons short of it now. They are commons, and they are in the
packs.

**The tower climbs to 26.** The Drowned Deacon waits at rung 25, milling her
own library on a schedule and casting it back from the grave. The
Marsh-Mother closes the climb at 26: she plants small things in the spring
and harvests Horrors in the autumn. Both are held to measured win-rate
floors, like every rung before them.

**A new deck in the shop.** Lanterns Below is a blue-black Drowned Deep deck:
cheap bodies hold the ground, the graveyard fills on schedule, and what slips
into it comes back as a Whisper.

**Opponents play the whole card now.** This release rebuilt how the AI reads
the game. It casts every kind of spell for what the spell does, where it used
to pick many of them by cost alone. It knows when passing is the right play.
It blocks and attacks correctly around Twin Blades, Sentinel and First Blade.
It sacrifices sensibly for Tithe, moves a Hauntlink to the creature that
wants it, and holds mana for a Duty worth waiting for. Draft opponents read
every mechanic on a card when they pick. Forty written-down behaviours are
tested, and every one passes.

**Six bosses were rebuilt on measured evidence.** Better opponents exposed
decks that had been winning on the other side's mistakes. Chrome Broodmother
could not block a single flier, and now she can. Kitsune Neon Tyrant had lost
her four Redline Queenpins to a conversion error, and has them back. The
Queen of the Lanterned Roof had nothing to play before turn three, and now
she does. Bastet, Brunhild and the Drowned Deacon were tuned the same way.
Every boss from rung 14 up clears her floor with a full margin. Eight floors
were raised, and the two new bosses took their first.

**Anonymous play stats, off in one tap.** Starting with 1.8 the game can send
short anonymous summaries of how it is played: which formats and colours get
played, how long duels run, which cards see play. Every number is rounded
into a broad range before it leaves your device. There is no name, no
account and no identifier, so nobody, including us, can tell which summaries
came from you. The first time you reach the main menu the game tells you
this, with the switch right there, before anything is sent. **What is sent**
lists every field. When the switch is off, the game makes no requests to the
stats service at all. The privacy policy is linked from the same panel.

**How we read those numbers.** As a rough guide, never as proof. The stats
service is public and can be spammed, and ad blockers drop some share of web
reports and none on desktop, so the two are not comparable. Card changes are
still decided the way they always have been, by thousands of measured matches
between the game's own opponents.

**Spell art shows her face.** Art for spells, artifacts and enchantments was
being cropped around the middle of the picture, which on 26 cards cut the
face out of the card window. The crop now finds the subject first. Twenty-nine
cards were reframed from their original illustrations and four were repainted.

**Fixes.** A chain of triggers that fed itself could lock a duel; it now
stops quietly. One card may now carry both Whispers and Tithe, and Cinderjaw
the Horror does. Opening an older save in a newer version could
reset a few one-time settings, such as the Darlings tutorial flag and a
deck's chosen card back; saves now keep them. The desktop app no longer logs
errors each time it sizes its window.
