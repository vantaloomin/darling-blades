# Patch 1.9: First Dawn

> *"Strike it and it gets angry. Strike it and miss, and it gets you."*
> A saying of the Fern clan

166 new cards from the morning the sun first rose, two new laws of the
valley, and two new bosses at the top of the tower. The cards lose their
flavor text and gain a taller picture. The art now loads only when a screen
asks for it, so the Collection opens at once. And a lot of quiet work went
into making the game easier to read and to play without leaning on colour.

## In this patch

- **New set:** First Dawn, 166 cards, with **Provoked** and **Hunt**
- **New across the whole game:** **Overcharge**, for when the board is full
- **The tower climbs to 28**, and the summit's Darlings decks were rebuilt
- **Card faces:** no more flavor text, and a taller art window
- **Art loads on demand:** the Collection opens at once, on far less memory
- **Older boosters cost less**, and they fill your collection faster
- **Opponents learned to hunt**, and Medium stopped wasting cards
- **Accessibility:** **Text size** and **High contrast** settings, and cues
  that never rely on colour alone
- A few balance changes, fixes, and the usual sweeping up

## New set: First Dawn

The Cradle is a green valley inside a ring of smoking mountains. The five
clans who live there believe the sun was born this morning, and that
everything alive is waking up to it. The ferns are taller than a woman, the
rivers run warm off the volcanoes, and the beasts are the largest things that
will ever live.

The clans are hunters, trackers, fire-keepers, seers and elders. The beasts
are raptor packs, horned herds, long-necks, armoured walls and tyrants, some
as Dinokin and some as plain Dinosaurs, with a few mammoths, sabre-cats and
cave bears among the Beastkin. It is sunlit, primal, and has teeth.

All 166 cards carry finished art, and so do the set's four new tokens:
Hatchling, Pack Raptor, Tar-Bones and Glider. First Dawn has its own
boosters (525 gold), a new theme deck in the shop, and six new achievements.

*"The herd crosses the Long Grass at first light. Nobody counts them. Nobody
needs to."*

### Provoked

> When this creature is dealt damage and survives, it does the listed effect.
> This triggers only once each turn.

What is struck and survives comes back angrier. 35 First Dawn cards carry it.

| Card | Cost | Body | It does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Walking Mountain** | {4}{G}{G} | 6/7 | Warding Gaze. `Provoked: Create two 1/1 Hatchling tokens.` |
| **Ember-Crest Tyrant** | {3}{R}{R} | 5/5 | Warcry. `Provoked: Deal 2 damage to target creature an opponent controls.` |
| **Hearth-Shield Maiden** | {W} | 1/4 | Bulwark. `Provoked: You gain 2 life.` A wall that thanks you for hitting it |

Your opponent decides whether to provoke your creatures, mostly. You can
also do it yourself: plenty of red and white cards deal 1 damage to a
creature *you* control, on purpose, and then make it better.

*"The old wall-beasts do not charge. They wait to be bitten."*

### Hunt

> Your creature and its prey each deal damage equal to their Attack to the
> other, at the same time. First Blade and Twin Blades don't apply to a Hunt.
> The prey is a creature an opponent controls, unless the card says
> otherwise. A creature with Bulwark cannot hunt. A creature that hunts when
> it arrives can't be cast unless it has prey.

A hunt is a fight both sides can lose. It is also green's first real
removal, and the cleanest way to provoke your own creature on your own terms:
if your hunter survives the prey's bite, she is Provoked. 21 First Dawn cards
can Hunt.

| Card | Cost | Body | It does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kesh, Raptor-Rider** | {2}{R} | 3/2 | Warcry. `Whenever this attacks, Hunt.` |
| **Korru, Eldest of the Trackers** | {3}{G}{G} | 4/5 | `{1}{G}, {T}: Hunt any other creature.` A Duty, every turn, for as long as she lasts |
| **Duel on the Ridge** | {2}{R} | Charm | `Target creature you control gets +2/+1 until Sunset, then it Hunts.` Removal in your opponent's combat |

*"She tracked it for three days. It tracked her for two. It was close."*

- **Oru, the Tyrant Queen**, {4}{R}{G}, 5/5. Overrun. `Other Dinokin
  creatures you control get +1/+1.` `Dinosaur creatures you control gain

  will meet her again, at the top of the tower.

### New theme deck: Hooves and Fire

A red-green Dinokin stampede, 500 gold in the shop. Every creature is an
honest body on a steady curve. Cheap Provoked creatures and Ember-Flick wake
each other up, Hunt and burn clear the blockers, the Herd-Caller gives the
whole pack Overrun, and two tyrants close it out.

### Six new achievements

From **First Footprints** (own a quarter of the set) to **The First Dawn**
(own all of it), plus **Nine Before Sunrise** for all nine Ultra Rares, **The
Great Hunt** for every Hunt card and **Do Not Wake Them** for every Provoked
card. That makes 114 in all, worth 5,600 more gold.

## New rule: Overcharge

There has always been a limit of 8 creatures on your side of the board, and
until now a token made past it simply vanished. First Dawn makes a great many
Hatchlings, so the limit now does something:

> If a token would be created while you control 8 creatures, it isn't.
> Instead, a token you control with the same name gets an Overcharge: +1/+1,
> up to 3 on one creature. With no such token, nothing happens. Overcharge
> isn't a Mark.

The duel history tells you either way: an Overcharge, or "Board full: no room
for" the token. Overcharge has its own entry in the Glossary.

One Starborne charm, **Overcharge the Hull**, was renamed **Vent the Reactor**
so that it stops sounding like a rule. Its cost and text are unchanged.

## The tower climbs to 28

**Rung 27: The Shepherdess of Giants**, *Strike the Herd and It Grows.*
"Tahla walks the long grass with a herd that outweighs a village. Every blow
you land wakes another calf, and every calf she keeps is one more wall
between you and her."

**Rung 28: The Tyrant Queen**, *The Last Thing That Hunts.*
"Oru is the biggest thing in the valley, and she knows it. Her trackers pick
the prey, her firekeepers wake the beasts, and whatever is still standing
when the hunt ends meets the queen herself."

The two new rungs pay 570 and 590 gold, so a full climb is now worth 9,210
gold before the daily first-win bonus. The tower has never had a green-white
boss before. It has one now, and she is very patient.

### Boss tuning

Win rates are each boss, on Hard, against the five starter decks over 1,000
measured games.

| Rung | Boss | Before | After | What was actually wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | **The Morrigan** | 41% | 65% | She had no two-drop and one kind of removal. Now she has cheap answers to fast decks |
| 13 | **Morgan of the Thorn Crown** | 47% | 54% | Slow curses where her early removal should be |
| 28 | **The Tyrant Queen** | 64% | 76% | Shipped rough, rebuilt before release into a fast red curve. The final rung now outranks the two below it |

The Violet Signal Queen (rung 24) was carrying two copies of a card that
needs Marked creatures, in a deck with no way to Mark anything. They are now
two counterspells that work.

### The summit, in Darlings

Until this patch the six bosses at the top of the tower, from Chrome
Broodmother up, played Darlings with decks of about sixty one-mana odds and
ends: trinkets, small life gains, and very little that could win. They now
play real decks built around their Darling, the way the bosses below them
already did. Rungs 23 to 26 go from winning 10 to 30% of their measured
Darlings games to between 55 and 58%. The Shepherdess of Giants, who used to
stall a good share of her games against one deck into a draw at the turn
limit, now finishes them.

## Card faces: no more flavor text

Flavor text came over from older card games and was not doing much here, so
it is gone from every card: the face, the enlarged view and the collection
detail alike. The room went to the art.

- **The art window is taller**, 216 pixels instead of 192 on the full-size
  card, so every card shows more of its picture: more room above the head,
  more of the scene around her.
- **The rules text keeps the whole box**, so far fewer cards have to shrink
  their text to fit.
- **324 older cards were re-framed** from their original illustrations so
  the head sits where the taller window wants it.
- The numbers in the grey cost circles, on target badges, on the Mark badge
  and on the pile counts are now drawn shapes rather than font text, so they
  stay crisp and properly centred at every size.

We will miss one or two of the jokes. The art will not miss them at all.

## Art loads when you need it

Until this patch the game still brought in every card illustration it owns,
all 1,500-odd of them, over the course of a session, and kept all of them in
memory. The menu opened quickly, but the Collection waited for the lot:
about 18 seconds on a fast connection and close to a minute on a slower one.

Now a card's art is fetched when a screen is about to show it, and art you
have moved away from is let go when memory runs short. The difference:

- **The Collection opens at once.** The first page's art arrives in under
  half a second on a computer, and in about a second and a half on a slow
  phone.
- **About half the graphics memory** on a computer, and about a third less
  on a phone.
- A card that is drawn before its art arrives fills in by itself when it
  does, portraits included.

You should not notice anything else, which is the point.

## The shop

Older expansion boosters now cost 450 gold, the same as the base set. The
three newest sets stay at 525.

Today that means Starborne, Drowned Deep and First Dawn at 525, and every
other booster at 450. When the next set arrives, the oldest of those three
steps down by itself.

Booster commons and rares now give you cards you don't own yet before
repeats. Once you own every common (or every rare) in a set, that slot draws
from the whole set again. Super-rare and higher slots already worked this way.
Prices and rewards are unchanged.

## Opponents learned to hunt

Every difficulty reads Hunt and Provoked. Hard plays out each possible hunt
before it picks the prey, counting who survives and who gets provoked.
Medium only hunts when its hunter wins the fight and lives. Easy never hunts
its own creatures on purpose, which is a kindness. Draft opponents know what
both mechanics are for when they pick.

**Things opponents used to do, and no longer do:**

- Hold team pumps like Stand as One, Red-Moon Rampage and Shieldwall Call
  for the whole game, on Medium. Medium now casts one after blocks when it
  saves a creature, kills one, or wins.
- Tap one of your creatures after combat, when it was about to untap anyway.
- Cast a Mark payoff with nothing on the board to Mark.
- Treat an extra land drop as worthless. It now casts its ramp early, when
  the mana is still worth having.
- Value a keyword the same on any creature it lands on.
- Forget to use a creature's Duty before combat, when tapping your blocker
  first was the whole point.

**And Easy calls her Darling on purpose now**, three to four times as often
on the first rungs of the tower. She was always allowed to. She just had not
been told it was a good idea.

Every difficulty still plays through the same hidden-information view you do.

## Accessibility

1.9's other headline is a game that is easier to read, and that never asks
you to tell two colours apart to make a choice.

**Cues that do not rely on colour.** All of these are always on:

- **Targets are numbered.** A target you pick shows a 1 or a 2, on a
  creature, a player's portrait or a card in a graveyard. A graveyard card
  you picked used to just fade, which looked like "unavailable".
- **Blockers and attackers are labelled.** A creature you assign to block
  lifts and wears a **Blocks** tab, and a creature that can attack wears an
  **Attack** tab. Declared attackers stay lifted for the whole of combat.
- **Stats say what happened to them.** A damaged creature shows a slash
  through its Attack and Defense, and stats raised or lowered from the
  printed card show up and down arrows, Marks included.
- **The keyboard target** has its own corner brackets, instead of borrowing
  a colour that already meant something else.
- **Selected tabs and filters** carry a gold bar, not just a different
  colour, and so does the pack you choose in Limited.
- **Rarity in words.** The card zoom and every full-card inspect now name a
  card's rarity, "Rarity: Super Special Rare", the way the pack-pull inspect
  already did.
- **Pack Opening tells rarities apart by shape too.** Face-down specials
  carry a small SR, SSR or UR tab, the runway bar labels each run with its
  tier, and a new variant is marked with a diamond where a new card keeps its
  star.

**Every screen was rebuilt to hold larger text.** Menus, the Shop, the Deck
Builder, the Collection, the Duel, Limited, Pack Opening, Achievements and
every dialog now lay out from the size of their text instead of fixed
positions, so they reflow rather than clip. At today's size they look the
same as before. Along the way this fixed a few things you could see already:

- The Shop's "Deck granted" message no longer runs a long deck name into its
  own title.
- Pack Opening's "tap to skip" and "tap to continue" sit fully on screen.
- Achievement titles wrap instead of being cut off.

**Settings has three tabs:** Game, Audio and Accessibility.

**Text size and high contrast.** The Accessibility tab now has **Text size**
(Standard, Large or Largest) and **High contrast** (brighter text and solid
panels; card art is unchanged). Both preview the moment you choose them.
Card faces do not change size: hover over a card, or hold it on a touch
screen, to read it up close.

**Darling Blades stays English only.** This work makes the English game
easier to read. It does not add other languages, and none are planned.

## Balance changes

| Card | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Black Tide Rising** (Drowned Deep) | {B}{B}: `All creatures get -3/-3 until Sunset.` | {B}{B}: `All creatures get -2/-2 until Sunset.` It was winning one deck far more games than a common should |

Three Starborne cards, Starborne Relay, Signal Cathedral and Umbral Antenna,
now say "Marked creatures" instead of "creatures with Marks". Same rule,
fewer words.

## Smaller things

- **Deck codes carry the whole deck.** A shared Standard or Darlings deck now
  brings its Warchest and its Darling with it. Older codes still import.
  Importing over a deck with unsaved changes asks first.
- **Land styles** can now be chosen for Standard and Darlings decks, not only
  for Constructed decks on a touch screen.
- **The Premium draft note** ("You drafted 45 cards...") stays for the whole
  Build step, even after a reload, instead of only on your first visit.
- **The Draft hub says what a free run pays**: "Pays 40g to 300g after three
  matches, by wins." Premium Draft says "Pays no gold."
- **The tutorial opponent** is Watch-Sergeant Alder by name as well as by
  face, instead of borrowing your own portrait.
- **Darling Blades is now for players 16 and over.** The terms of service and
  privacy policy say so from this patch on.

## Bug fixes

- Fixed an issue where a Foresee that found an empty deck threw away the rest
  of its effect. The draw that should have decked a player now happens, and
  so does the loss.
- Fixed an issue where an opponent's portrait could keep its loading
  placeholder for the rest of the session.

**Replays** saved on 1.8.5 can't be played back on 1.9.
